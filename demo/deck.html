What's new
01
Sealed heirs — heirs are keccak commitments, not addresses. Nobody can see or target them. A physical claim card is the only key.
02
Guardian veto — an optional watchdog can cancel a premature claim. They can never touch funds; the contract forbids it.
03
Every action is a heartbeat — depositing, editing heirs, sealing a letter all reset the timer. No ritual check-ins.
04
Specific NFT bequests — "my Azuki goes to Alice", enforced on-chain, independent of percentage splits.
05
Sealed letters — AES-256 encrypted in the browser, ciphertext on-chain, passphrase whispered off-chain. Last words survive.
06
Pull-based payouts — heirs withdraw their own shares; shares are computed on frozen balances, so late claims can't dilute early ones.

Who it's for

Every long-term self-custody holder is a single accident away from burning their family's savings. Ethernal turns "telling your heirs the seed phrase" — today's only option, and a terrible one — into an enforceable on-chain will. Same primitive extends to DAO treasuries, founder key succession, and multisig disaster recovery.
